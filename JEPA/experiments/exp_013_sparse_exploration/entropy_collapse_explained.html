Why a pure-exploration policy collapses to deterministic

A self-contained explanation of the entropy collapse in the intrinsic-reward PPO agent.

The setup (everything you need)

The agent has a policy π(a|s) (probabilities over actions) and a critic V(s) (a learned estimate of future reward from state s). There is no task reward — the only reward is an intrinsic novelty signal r (high in unfamiliar states). PPO improves the policy by the advantage:

A(s,a) = R(s,a) − V(s),   R = Σl γl rt+l  (the discounted return)

Read A as “was this action better than the critic expected?” PPO raises π(a|s) for actions with positive advantage and lowers it for negative — and adds an entropy bonus that pushes the policy back toward uniform/random. Whether the policy stays random or collapses to one action is the tug-of-war between these two.

Two more facts about this setup that matter a lot:

  • Advantages are normalised per mini-batch: PPO subtracts the batch mean, A ← (A−mean)/std. So a uniform positive bias is removed — only the relative structure of advantages drives learning.
  • Returns are non-episodic with γ=0.99: the sum R is not reset at death, so it accumulates over a ~100-step effective horizon. Returns are therefore ~100× the per-step reward — large, and spiky when novelty bursts.

The paradox

Novelty reward is supposed to keep the agent exploring. Yet the policy entropy crashes from maximum (ln 4 ≈ 1.386, i.e. uniform over 4 actions) to ≈ 0 — it commits to a deterministic loop and stops exploring. Why?

The mechanism, step by step

1. Returns spike. Novelty is bursty and non-stationary (it jumps when the agent reaches a new region, or as features shift). Because returns accumulate over the long non-episodic horizon, a burst makes R jump hard (measured: R ran 3.9 → 8.4 → 14.0). measured
2. The critic lags — and lags unevenly. V(s) was fit to the past, lower returns, so it under-predicts the spike: R − V > 0 (measured mean R − V = +0.76, positive on 71% of updates). Crucially it under-predicts most in the high-novelty region the agent is currently visiting. measured
3. Per-batch normalisation removes the offset but keeps the structure. Subtracting the batch mean kills the uniform part of that +0.76. What survives is relative: transitions in the high-novelty region have above-average advantage → positive after normalisation; everything else → negative. mechanism
4. This is the answer to “how does a return spike collapse the policy?” PPO now raises the probability of the actions that led into the high-novelty region (positive relative advantage) and lowers all others. The policy concentrates toward that region.
5. Rich-get-richer feedback. A more concentrated policy samples that region even more → the critic keeps lagging the (sustained, bursty) novelty there → those same actions keep getting above-average advantage → probability keeps rising → entropy → 0. The entropy bonus opposes this, but at c_entropy=0.01 it is too weak to overcome the structured advantage. (Raising it to 0.05 stops the collapse — measured, but it's a band-aid: a big enough spike still wins.)
6. Trapped, then slow recovery. Once near-deterministic, the agent cannot sample alternatives, so it cannot discover that other actions are now more novel — the advantage signal can't redirect a policy that isn't exploring. Only when the fixated region's novelty finally decays does R − V flip negative, slowly re-injecting randomness (the partial entropy recovery we saw).

Net: bursty non-stationary return + a lagging, single critic → a structured (region-specific) positive advantage → the policy commits to the region it happened to find → exploration dies. The reward being “novelty” is incidental; what matters is that it is bursty and the critic can't track it.

What “value estimate” and “clip” mean here — and why clip alone isn't enough

“Value estimate” = V(s), and the regression target it chases, the return R. The spike is in R; the lag is V failing to track it.

So your instinct is correct on both counts: (a) a reward clip helps with transient spikes but cannot stop a sustained region's reward from accumulating into a large return under the long non-episodic horizon; and (b) the non-episodic GAE is a prime amplifier — it extends the horizon across episodes, inflating R and therefore the lag. The clean fixes target the return magnitude (shorter/episodic horizon), the critic's tracking speed, or — as insurance — the entropy floor.

One-line summary: the intrinsic return is bursty and (under non-episodic γ=0.99) large; a single critic lags it unevenly; per-batch advantage normalisation turns that uneven lag into a relative positive advantage for whatever region the agent is in; PPO reinforces the path there, the policy concentrates, samples it more, and the loop closes — entropy → 0. Clipping the per-step reward only tames transient spikes; bounding the return (shorter/episodic horizon) and an entropy floor are what actually break the loop.
