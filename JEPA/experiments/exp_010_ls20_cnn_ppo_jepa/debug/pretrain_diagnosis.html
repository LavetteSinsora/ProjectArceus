Diagnosing the JEPA pre-training run (exp_010_2_jepa_random_pretrain)

A research-scientist post-mortem of the offline JEPA encoder pre-train. Every number here is read directly from two committed artifacts that cross-validate each other: the logged runs/jepa_pretrain_20260530_035358/metrics.jsonl and jepa_pretrained/encoder_final.pt. Loss = MSE(pred(ht,a), sg(ht+1)) + CE(IDM(ht,ht+1),a), trunk_dim 256, 4 actions, Adam 3e-4, batch 256, stop-grad target, encoder output post-ReLU. Early-stop on val_jepa (patience 4, min_delta 0.002, cap 40 epochs).
TL;DR — the pre-training is essentially a no-op, killed after one epoch by a brittle criterion. The forward (state-prediction) validation loss rises right after epoch 1 (1.35 → 2.23 → 2.31) and never gets back below its epoch-1 value, so the patience-4 early-stop fires at epoch 5 and the saved encoder_final.pt is literally the epoch-1 model (verified: its stored epoch=1, best_val_jepa=1.3513). One epoch moves the encoder only ~29% (relative L2) off random init, and almost entirely in direction: its fc.weight norm barely changes (22.6→23.8) and its fc.bias stays essentially zero (0→0.045). So the "JEPA-pretrained" encoder handed to PPO is a lightly-perturbed random network — which is the most likely upstream reason JEPA-init ≈ random-init in the downstream phantom-advantages report.

2 · Consequence: the deployed encoder is a one-epoch model

The early-stop watches val_jepa: epoch 1 (1.351) is the global minimum; epochs 2–5 are all above it, so the "bad" counter hits patience 4 and training halts at epoch 5. _save_encoder only runs on an improvement, i.e. only at epoch 1.

Verified directly from encoder_final.pt: epoch = 1, best_val_jepa = 1.3513 (identical to the epoch-1 row above). The two artifacts agree, so this is not a logging quirk — the shipped encoder really is the epoch-1 snapshot.

3 · What did that one epoch actually change? (the "norm" question)

encoder_final (epoch 1)23.770.045~0.29 (rel. L2)
No weight-space norm explosion at this checkpoint. The output-layer weight norm barely moves (22.6→23.8) and the bias stays essentially zero (0.045). The change is ~29% but it is almost pure rotation, not growth — the encoder is a direction-perturbed random network. If the representation does have a large feature-space norm in practice, it is inherited from the orthogonal init / conv stack, not grown by pre-training — at least not by the single epoch that was kept.

4 · The two predictors interact badly (the real failure mode)

  • The action-predictor (IDM) saturates instantly. val_idm_acc is already 0.915 at epoch 1 and stays 0.915–0.918 forever; its loss is ~0.14 and flat. After epoch 1 it supplies essentially no new gradient to the encoder.
  • The state-predictor (forward JEPA) dominates and is unstable. Its loss is ~10× the IDM loss and it increases for two epochs. And it is the sole early-stop criterion.
  • Net effect: the one objective that could keep shaping the encoder (forward prediction) is the one that misbehaves and then trips the kill-switch, while the satisfied action head can't rescue it. Pre-training stops before the encoder learns anything substantial.

5 · Why does the forward val loss rise? (hypothesis — not confirmed)

The target is a stop-gradient of the encoder's own next-state embedding with no EMA target and no normalization. As the encoder reorganizes in the first epochs, that target is non-stationary (its scale and geometry drift), so the raw MSE to it can grow even while the predictor is tracking it — a classic moving-target JEPA instability. A raw-MSE patience criterion then mistakes target drift for "no progress."

Honestly, this cannot be confirmed from the committed artifacts. Epochs 2–4 weights were never saved (the early-stop only saves on improvement), and the random buffer + the LS20 env are not committed (third_party.ls20 lived only on Colab; data/random_buffer.npz is absent). So I could measure neither the per-epoch feature norm ‖h‖ nor effective rank that would prove/refute the target-drift mechanism.

6 · Recommended experiment to settle it

  • Re-run the pre-train logging, per epoch, the mean ‖h‖, the per-dim std, and the effective rank of the encoder features on the val split (the missing measurements above).
  • Replace the raw-MSE early-stop with a scale-invariant target (EMA / stop-grad-normalized target, or a variance-/covariance-regularized loss à la VICReg), or at least make the stop criterion a normalized/cosine metric — then check whether val "loss" still rises.
  • Ablate: drop the IDM head, drop the forward head — see which one drives any norm growth and which one the early-stop should track.
