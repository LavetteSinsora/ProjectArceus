Diagnosing representation collapse in a JEPA world model

A loss curve dropped 20×. The representations died. Here is how we found out.

Setup

We train an RL agent on a small grid-world puzzle game. The agent sees a 64×64 frame of colored tiles and chooses one of four moves. Instead of feeding pixels into a policy, we train a world model alongside it: an encoder produces a compact latent state, a predictor learns to roll that state forward under an action, and a small policy network reads the latent and picks an action. This is the JEPA recipe — predict in latent space, not pixel space.

The exact loss

We use flow matching. Each batch sample draws a random τ ∈ [0,1], forms a linear interpolation, and asks the predictor to recover the endpoint:

xτ = (1 − τ) · ht + τ · ht+1   ·   ℒ = ‖ ht+1 − predictor(xτ, τ, a) ‖²

One τ per sample. No inner accumulation. Gradient flows backward into the predictor and (via xτ) into ht and the online encoder. It is explicitly stopped at ht+1.

Symptoms

After ~85K training steps, the dashboard told a confusing story. Loss was falling cleanly, but several other numbers were not behaving like a healthy run.

A loss that drops while diversity metrics deteriorate is a classic warning for self-supervised learning: the model may have found the trivial fixed point (constant outputs) but the loss does not directly punish it.

Probe 1 — Where is the gradient going?

The "encoder.perceiver" gradient norm of 11.5 is an aggregate over ~800K parameters. We re-loaded the checkpoint, replayed one training step, and walked every named parameter.

We then split the batch by whether the encoder used learned placeholder queries (start-of-episode) or recurrent queries (mid-episode), and computed the loss and gradient separately for each subset:

Diagnostic: the recurrent subset has tiny loss but huge gradient. That signature — loss collapsing toward a degenerate fixed point with sharp local Jacobians — means the optimizer is burning energy maintaining a near-constant output rather than learning structure.

Probe 2 — What do the representations actually look like?

We measured effective rank of the stored latent representations across 1024 transitions. Effective rank is the entropy of the normalized singular values: a soft count of "how many directions carry meaningful variance." For an N×D matrix it is bounded above by min(N, D).

We measured it three ways. Flattened: stack all 4 latent tokens into a 512-dim row (max rank 512). Per token: take a single token's 128-dim slice (max rank 128). Within state: the 4 tokens of one sample (max rank 4).

Every latent has the same length to four decimal places, because the output LayerNorm forces it. The collapse is entirely angular — and the angles have collapsed too.

Why it happened

The trivial fixed point — encoder outputs a constant, predictor learns identity, loss → 0 — is always reachable by this loss alone. The standard guards (stop-gradient on ht+1, EMA target encoder) make it harder to reach but do not eliminate it as a basin. In our run, four compounding factors pulled the model in:

  1. The output LayerNorm hides one symptom. Collapsing encoders normally produce shrinking norms; ours can't, because the LN at the Perceiver output forces ‖h‖ ≈ √128 ≈ 11.3 regardless of input. The collapse is only visible angularly.
  2. Adjacent frames are nearly identical. A predictor that learns "output ≈ input" gets most of the loss right. ODE cosine 0.998 confirms it. Once this is learned, the encoder is free to collapse without penalty.
  3. Curiosity reward goes to zero with the encoder. When all states map to the same latent, predictor error is zero everywhere — the policy receives a flat reward landscape and cannot push the encoder back toward diversity.
  4. Nothing penalizes within-state token redundancy. The cheapest way for the 4 Perceiver queries to produce 4 outputs is to emit the same vector four times. Within-state effective rank never exceeded 1.6/4.

The EMA target encoder tracked the collapse rather than preventing it: relative parameter distance between online and target dropped from 1.7% (step 5K) to 0.5% (step 80K). The EMA buffers noise in the online encoder's updates; it does not refuse direction.

Mitigations, in leverage order

#InterventionWhat it fixes
1Variance regularizer on pre-LN latents (VICReg)Directly penalizes per-dim std < 1 — fights the angular collapse the LN currently hides.
2Covariance off-diagonal penaltyPushes the 128 dims toward statistical independence — raises eff rank.
3Within-state token diversity lossPenalize cosine similarity between the 4 latent tokens at the same time-step.
4Reward = error / latent_movementRemoves the "flat-encoder gives zero reward" loophole — policy regains push-back against collapse.

Takeaway

A monotone loss curve is not evidence that a self-supervised representation is learning. The training loss in this setup can be satisfied by a constant function. The diagnostics that actually predicted the failure were per-token effective rank (sees collapse in the manifold), norm standard deviation (sees the LayerNorm hiding magnitude collapse), and per-sub-module gradient breakdown (sees one block hogging 99.99% of the gradient). These are the signals worth plotting first next time.
